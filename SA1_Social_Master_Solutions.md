# Geography — Chapter 3: The Climates of India (Pages 38–55)

### 🌍 Conceptual Synopsis & Quick Revision
- **Weather vs Climate:**
  - **Weather:** The condition of the atmosphere at a specific place and time (changes daily or hourly).
  - **Climate:** The average weather pattern of a large region observed over a long period (usually 30+ years).
- **Factors Influencing India's Climate:**
  1. **Latitude:** The Tropic of Cancer divides India into tropical south and subtropical north.
  2. **Altitude:** Places at higher altitudes (hills/mountains like Shimla and Ooty) are cooler because air becomes thinner.
  3. **Distance from Sea:** Coastal areas have a **moderate (equable)** climate due to sea and land breezes; inland areas experience extreme temperatures.
  4. **Relief & Mountains:** The Himalayas block cold arctic winds and trap monsoon winds; Western Ghats cause heavy orographic rainfall.
  5. **Monsoon Winds:** Seasonal reversing winds that bring the majority of India's annual rainfall.
- **Climatic Zones of India:**
  - **Alpine Zone:** Cold, snowy winters (Ladakh, Kashmir, high Himalayas).
  - **Subtropical Zone:** Warm summers and cool winters (Northern Plains).
  - **Tropical Wet Zone:** Warm and humid with heavy rainfall (Western coastal plains, Kerala).
  - **Arid & Semi-Arid Zones:** Very hot and dry with scanty rainfall (Thar Desert in Rajasthan).
- **Monsoon Cycles:**
  - **Southwest Monsoon (June–September):** Moisture-laden winds blow from Arabian Sea & Bay of Bengal onto mainland India.
  - **Northeast / Retreating Monsoon (October–November):** Winds blow from land back to the sea, bringing rain to Tamil Nadu coast.
- **Climate Disasters & Conservation:**
  - Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs), landslides, cyclones, and forest fires.
  - **Mitigation:** Planting trees, using renewable energy (solar, wind).
  - **Resilience & Adaptation:** Stronger construction, disaster warning systems.

### Descriptive Questions — Chapter 3

#### A. Short Answer Questions (1 to 2 sentences)
1. **What is meant by climate? Write in one or two short sentences.**
   - **Answer:** Climate is the average weather condition of a large area recorded over a long period of time (typically 30 years or more).
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* శీతోష్ణస్థితి (Climate) అంటే ఒక పెద్ద ప్రాంతంలో కనీసం 30 ఏళ్ల పాటు సగటున ఉండే వాతావరణ పరిస్థితుల అధ్యయనం.
2. **How would you define 'weather' in simple words?**
   - **Answer:** Weather is the day-to-day condition of the atmosphere at a particular place regarding temperature, humidity, rainfall, and wind.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* వాతావరణం (Weather) అంటే ఒక రోజు లేదా గంటల వ్యవధిలో ఉష్ణోగ్రత, వర్షం, గాలిలో వచ్చే స్వల్పకాలిక మార్పులు.

   - **Answer:** They are called the **Monsoon winds** (specifically the Southwest Monsoon winds).

#### B. Brief Descriptive Questions (3 to 4 sentences — Simple & Kid-Friendly)
1. **How does altitude influence the climate and temperature of a region?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - As we go higher above sea level, the air becomes thinner.
     - Thin air holds less heat, so temperatures drop as altitude increases.
     - For every 1,000 meters rise, temperature drops by about 6.5°C.
     - This is why hill stations like Shimla and Ooty are pleasantly cool in summer while nearby plains are hot.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* సముద్ర మట్టం నుంచి పైకి వెళ్లేకొద్దీ గాలి సాంద్రత తగ్గి ఉష్ణోగ్రత పడిపోతుంది. అందుకే సిమ్లా, ఊటీ వంటి కొండ ప్రాంతాలు ఎండాకాలంలో కూడా చల్లగా ఉంటాయి.

2. **In what ways do winds affect temperature, rainfall and humidity levels?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - Winds blowing from the ocean carry water vapor, which increases humidity and causes rainfall (e.g., Monsoon winds).
     - Winds blowing from hot deserts make places hot and dry (e.g., 'Loo' winds in North India).
     - Cold winds blowing from snowy mountains lower the temperature of nearby regions.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* సముద్రం నుంచి వీచే గాలులు తేమను తెచ్చి వర్షాన్ని కురిపిస్తాయి; ఎడారి నుంచి వీచే గాలులు (లూ) వేడిని పెంచుతాయి; మంచు కొండల నుంచి వచ్చే గాలులు చల్లదనాన్నిస్తాయి.

3. **What are the features of a tropical wet climate found in coastal India?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - It has warm and pleasant temperatures throughout the year with very little seasonal change.
     - The region experiences high humidity and heavy rainfall from the southwest monsoon.
     - It supports lush green vegetation, dense forests, and crops like coconut and paddy (e.g., Kerala coast).
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* ఉష్ణమండల తేమ శీతోష్ణస్థితిలో ఏడాది పొడవునా వెచ్చగా, గాలిలో తేమ ఎక్కువగా ఉంటుంది. విస్తారంగా వర్షాలు కురిసి కొబ్బరి, వరి పంటలకు అనుకూలంగా ఉంటుంది.

4. **Mention two natural and two human-made causes of forest fires.**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Natural Causes:**
       1. Lightning strikes hitting dry trees or dry grass.
       2. High summer heat with friction of dry bamboo rubbing in strong winds.
     - **Human-Made Causes:**
       1. Carelessly throwing burning cigarette butts or matchsticks in forests.
       2. Leaving campfires unextinguished or setting deliberate fires for land clearing.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* సహజ కారణాలు: పిడుగుపాటు, ఎండ వేడికి ఎండిన వెదురు రాపిడి. మానవ తప్పిదాలు: ఆర్పని క్యాంప్ ఫైర్లు, వెలిగించిన సిగరెట్ పీకలు పారవేయడం.

5. **Why do places near the ocean have milder temperatures than places far away from it?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - Water heats up and cools down much slower than land.
     - During the day, cool **sea breeze** blows towards land to keep it cool; at night, **land breeze** balances the temperature.
     - This moderating effect prevents extreme heat in summer and severe cold in winter (equable climate).
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* నేల కంటే నీరు నెమ్మదిగా వేడెక్కి, నెమ్మదిగా చల్లబడుతుంది. పగటిపూట సముద్రపు గాలులు, రాత్రి వేళ భూ గాలులు వీచి ఉష్ణోగ్రతను సమతుల్యంగా ఉంచుతాయి.

#### C. Long Descriptive Questions (5 to 7 sentences)
1. **What is the difference between weather and climate?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Weather** describes short-term atmospheric conditions of a small area, while **climate** describes long-term averages over a large region.
     - Weather can change within minutes or hours (e.g., a sunny morning turning rainy in the afternoon).
     - Climate remains stable and predictable over decades (usually 30 years or more).
     - Weather elements include daily temperature, rainfall, wind, and sunshine.
     - Climate tells us the general type of environment, such as polar, arid, or tropical.
     - Knowing the weather helps in daily planning (what clothes to wear), while climate guides agriculture, architecture, and clothing traditions.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* వాతావరణం (Weather) రోజువారీగా లేదా గంటల వ్యవధిలో మారే చిన్న ప్రాంతపు పరిస్థితి. శీతోష్ణస్థితి (Climate) కనీసం 30 ఏళ్ల సగటు ఆధారంగా నిర్ణయించే విస్తృత ప్రాంత శాశ్వత నమూనా.

2. **Explain how both latitude and distance from the sea affect climate.**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Latitude Effect:** Places near the Equator receive direct overhead sun rays, making them hot. As we move towards the poles, sun rays fall at a slant and spread over a wider area, making high latitudes cold. In India, the Tropic of Cancer divides the country into a warm tropical south and a subtropical north.
     - **Distance from the Sea:** Land heats and cools faster than water. Coastal regions (like Mumbai and Chennai) enjoy a moderate climate with small temperature differences between summer and winter. In contrast, inland areas far from the sea (like Delhi and Nagpur) experience extreme climates—scorching hot summers and freezing cold winters.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* అక్షాంశాల (Latitude) వల్ల భూమధ్యరేఖ వద్ద సూర్యకిరణాలు నిలువుగా పడి వేడిగానూ, ధ్రువాల వైపు వాలుగా పడి చల్లగానూ ఉంటుంది. సముద్రానికి దూరం పెరిగేకొద్దీ తీవ్ర ఉష్ణోగ్రతలు (Extreme Climate) ఏర్పడతాయి.

3. **What role do monsoon winds play in affecting India's climate?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - The word 'Monsoon' comes from the Arabic word *Mawsim*, meaning season.
     - In summer, the Indian landmass heats up rapidly, creating a low-pressure area that draws moist winds from the ocean (Southwest Monsoon).
     - These winds bring over 70% of India's annual rainfall between June and September.
     - They give life to Indian agriculture, refill rivers, lakes, and ground water, and lower the scorching summer heat.
     - In winter, the winds reverse and blow from land to sea (Northeast Monsoon), bringing rainfall to the Coromandel coast in Tamil Nadu.
     - A good monsoon brings agricultural prosperity, while monsoon failure causes drought.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* రుతుపవనాలు భారతదేశానికి జీవనాడి. నైరుతి రుతుపవనాలు జూన్-సెప్టెంబర్ మధ్య 70% కంటే ఎక్కువ వర్షాన్ని అందించి వ్యవసాయాన్ని, నదులను నింపుతాయి.

4. **How does climate influence the economic activities and livelihood of people in India?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Agriculture:** Farming depends directly on the monsoon; crops like paddy and sugarcane need heavy rain, whereas wheat and pulses grow in cooler, drier months.
     - **Occupations:** Coastal people engage in fishing and maritime trade; mountain people practice terrace farming, apple orchards, and animal grazing.
     - **Tourism:** Snow in Kashmir and Himachal Pradesh attracts winter tourists, while beaches of Goa attract tourists in winter months.
     - **Energy Production:** Abundant rainfall allows hydroelectric dams to produce electricity, while sunny regions in Rajasthan generate massive solar power.
     - **Housing and Clothing:** Houses in high-rainfall areas have slanting roofs; people in cold regions wear woollens, while coastal people wear light cottons.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* శీతోష్ణస్థితి మన ఆహారం, దుస్తులు, గృహ నిర్మాణం, వ్యవసాయం మరియు ఉపాధిని ప్రత్యక్షంగా నిర్ణయిస్తుంది.

5. **What are the major causes and visible effects of climate change across the world?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Causes:** Burning fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) releases greenhouse gases like carbon dioxide; widespread deforestation reduces trees that absorb CO2; industrial pollution and excessive energy use.
     - **Visible Effects:** Global temperatures are rising, leading to heatwaves and melting glaciers in the Arctic and Himalayas.
     - Rising sea levels threaten low-lying coastal cities and island nations.
     - Weather extremes have increased, causing severe droughts in some areas and devastating floods in others.
     - Forest fires and unseasonal cyclones have become more frequent and destructive.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* శిలాజ ఇంధనాలు మండించడం, అడవుల నరికివేత వల్ల గ్లోబల్ వార్మింగ్ పెరిగి మంచు కరిగి సముద్ర మట్టాలు పెరగడం, తుఫానులు, వరదలు రావడం జరుగుతోంది.

#### C. Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS)
- **Question:** *Why is Chennai warm or hot throughout the year while Leh is cold?*
  - **Answer:**
    1. **Latitude:** Chennai is located close to the Equator (low latitude), receiving direct sun rays all year, while Leh is far to the north (high latitude).
    2. **Altitude:** Chennai is at sea level, while Leh is high in the Himalayas at over 3,500 meters altitude where the thin air is extremely cold.
    3. **Proximity to Sea:** Chennai is on the coast where warm sea air keeps temperatures warm, while Leh is landlocked in a high-altitude cold desert.

#### D. Subject Integration Questions
1. **Science Integration:** *Explain how uneven heating of land and water causes seasonal winds.*
   - **Answer:** Land heats up much faster than water during the day and summer. The air above the land becomes hot, expands, and rises, creating a **low-pressure area**. Cooler, heavier air from the ocean (high-pressure area) rushes in to take its place. This movement of air from sea to land creates sea breezes and seasonal monsoon winds.
2. **English Integration:** *A Day Without Rain — Summary paragraph:*
   - **Answer:** On a blistering summer day without rain, the earth turns parched and cracked. Dust rises with every gust of wind, plants droop their leaves, and animals search desperately for shade. The absence of rain reminds us that water is the lifeblood of our environment, and every drop is precious for farmers and nature alike.

# History — Chapter 8: The Age of Reorganisation (Pages 106–121)

### 🏛️ Conceptual Synopsis & Quick Revision
- **The Period of Reorganisation (c. 185 BCE – 300 CE):**
  - Followed the collapse of the mighty Mauryan Empire.
  - Characterized by the rise of energetic regional kingdoms and foreign ruling dynasties across the Indian subcontinent.
- **Key Regional Dynasties:**
  - **The Shunga Dynasty (North India):** Founded by **Pushyamitra Shunga** (185 BCE); revived the Vedic ritual of *Ashvamedha Yajna*; renovated Buddhist stupas like **Bharhut** and **Sanchi**.
  - **The Chedi Dynasty of Kalinga (Odisha):** Led by the great **King Kharavela**; inscribed his 13-year achievements on the rock face of **Hathigumpha** (Elephant Cave) at Udayagiri; patronized Jainism while protecting all faiths.
  - **The Satavahanas (Deccan):** Ruled Maharashtra, Telangana, and Andhra Pradesh; famous rulers include **Gautamiputra Satakarni** (honoured his mother Gautami Balashri); minted coins with ships symbolizing maritime trade with the Roman Empire; constructed rock-cut chaityas at Karla and Kanheri.
  - **The Kushanas (North-West):** Originating from Central Asia; most celebrated emperor was **King Kanishka**; controlled the wealthy Silk Route; patronized the 4th Buddhist Council; started the **Shaka Samvat** era (78 CE); developed Gandhara and Mathura art.
  - **The Southern Dynasties (Sangam Age):**
    - **Cholas:** Ruler **Karikala Chola** built the world's oldest functional stone dam, the **Grand Anicut (Kallanai)** across River Cauvery.
    - **Pandyas:** Ruled from Madurai; famous for pearl fishing and patronizing three great literary assemblies (**Sangams**).
    - **Cheras:** Ruled Kerala and western Tamil Nadu; traded extensively with Rome.

# Civics — Chapter 13: India, a Home to Many (Pages 186–197)

### 🤝 Conceptual Synopsis & Quick Revision
- **Core Philosophy:**
  - ***Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam*:** An ancient Sanskrit phrase from the Maha Upanishad meaning **"The World is One Family"**.
  - India has historically been a land of peace, acceptance, and a safe refuge for persecuted communities from around the world.
- **Refugee & Migrant Communities Sheltered in India:**
  1. **The Jews:**
     - **Bene Israel:** Shipwrecked ancestors landed on the Konkan Coast (Maharashtra) over 2,000 years ago; adopted Marathi language while preserving Jewish customs.
     - **Cochin Jews:** Settled on the Malabar Coast (Kerala); welcomed by the Hindu King of Kochi who granted them land right next to his palace to build the historic **Paradesi Synagogue** (1568).
  2. **The Syrian Christians:**
     - Arrived in Kerala in the 1st century CE following the arrival of **St. Thomas** the Apostle; lived in complete harmony and were granted royal copper plates (*Sasanams*) by Chera kings.
  3. **The Parsis (Zoroastrians):**
     - Fled religious persecution in Persia (Iran) in the 8th century CE and landed at **Sanjan, Gujarat**.
     - **The Milk & Sugar Legend:** King **Jadi Rana** showed their leader a bowl full of milk to symbolize his land was full. The Parsi priest added a spoonful of **sugar** to the milk, showing they would sweeten and enrich Indian society without displacing anyone.
     - Built Fire Temples (*Atash Behram*) and contributed greatly to India's industry, education, and freedom struggle (e.g., Tatas, Dadabhai Naoroji, Homi Bhabha).
  4. **The Baha'i Community:**
     - Originated in Persia in the 19th century preaching unity of all religions; found peace in India; built the iconic **Lotus Temple** in New Delhi.
  5. **Polish Children (World War II):**
     - **"The Good Maharaja"** Digvijaysinhji Jadeja of Nawanagar (Jamnagar, Gujarat) gave shelter, food, and schools to nearly 1,000 orphaned Polish children escaping war in 1942–1946. Poland built a memorial in Warsaw in his honor.
  6. **Tibetan Refugees:**
     - In 1959, following political turmoil in Tibet, the **14th Dalai Lama** escaped to India across the Himalayas.
     - Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru granted them asylum; major settlements were established at **Dharamshala** (Himachal Pradesh) and **Bylakuppe** (Karnataka).

# Economics — Chapter 17: From Barter to Money (Pages 236–243)

### 💰 Conceptual Synopsis & Quick Revision
- **The Barter System:**
  - Direct exchange of goods and services for other goods and services without using money.
- **5 Major Limitations of Barter:**
  1. **Lack of Double Coincidence of Wants:** Both parties must want exactly what the other has to offer at the same time.
  2. **Lack of a Common Measure of Value:** There is no standard unit to calculate how many chickens equal one cow or a sack of wheat.
  3. **Difficulty in Storing Wealth:** Goods like vegetables, fruits, and milk spoil quickly; storing cattle is expensive.
  4. **Indivisibility of Goods:** Live animals cannot be divided into smaller pieces without killing them.
  5. **Difficulty in Deferred (Future) Payments:** Hard to repay loans in goods because their quality and value change over time.
- **Evolution of Money:**
  1. **Commodity Money:** Grains, salt, cowrie shells, cattle.
  2. **Metallic Money:** Gold, silver, and copper punch-marked coins (introduced around 600 BCE).
  3. **Dynastic Coins:** Indo-Greek portraits, Satavahana ships, Chola tigers, Chalukya/Vijayanagara **Varaha** (boar) coins.
  4. **Paper Currency:** Authorized by central governments (Reserve Bank of India established in 1935).
  5. **Bank & Plastic Money:** Cheques, Demand Drafts, Credit and Debit cards.
  6. **Digital Money:** Net banking, UPI (Unified Payments Interface), mobile wallets, QR codes.
- **The Indian Rupee Symbol (₹):**
  - Designed by **D. Udaya Kumar** in 2010.
  - Combines the Devanagari letter **'र'** and the Roman capital letter **'R'**, with horizontal stripes symbolizing the Indian tricolor flag.
- **Living Example of Barter in Modern India:**
  - **Jonbeel Mela** in Morigaon district, Assam: An annual centuries-old harvest fair where hill tribes exchange herbs and ginger for plains rice cakes and fish without money!

   - **Simple Answer:**

     - Modern governments introduced lightweight paper notes and bank cheques.
     - Today, money has transformed into electronic pulses—cards, mobile wallets, and instant UPI payments (**digital money**).
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* డబ్బు మొదట్లో ధాన్యం, గవ్వలుగా మొదలై.. తర్వాత బంగారు, వెండి నాణేలుగా మారి.. ఆపై కాగితపు కరెన్సీగా, నేడు యూపీఐ (UPI), డిజిటల్ మనీగా మారింది.

3. **Explain the problem of divisibility in barter.**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - Many valuable items cannot be broken into smaller pieces without destroying their value.
     - For example, if a farmer owns a live cow and only needs a small bag of salt, he cannot divide the cow in half without killing it.
     - Money solves this problem completely because currency comes in small, exact denominations (like ₹1, ₹5, ₹10, ₹100).
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* బార్టర్ పద్ధతిలో ఆవు వంటి ప్రాణం ఉన్న వస్తువులను విభజించలేము. ఆవును సగం కోస్తే చనిపోతుంది. కానీ డబ్బును చిన్న మొత్తాలుగా (₹1, ₹5) సులభంగా విభజించవచ్చు.

4. **What is digital money?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - Digital money is money that exists only in electronic computer systems and has no physical paper or metal form.
     - It allows people to transfer funds instantly using bank apps, internet banking, credit cards, or UPI.
     - It is safe, quick, convenient, and can be used 24/7 from anywhere in the world.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* డిజిటల్ మనీ అంటే కాగితం, నాణేలు లేకుండా కంప్యూటర్, మొబైల్ సిస్టమ్స్ ద్వారా UPI, నెట్ బ్యాంకింగ్ సాయంతో తక్షణమే బదిలీ చేసే ఎలక్ట్రానిక్ డబ్బు.

#### C. Long Descriptive Questions (5 to 7 sentences)
1. **What were the limitations of the barter system?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Lack of Double Coincidence of Wants:** Both traders must simultaneously need what the other wants to sell, which rarely happens.
     - **Absence of a Common Measure of Value:** There was no fixed pricing unit to determine how much rice equals one sheep or a pair of shoes.
     - **Difficulty in Storing Value:** Perishable goods like fruits, vegetables, fish, and milk rot quickly, while storing cattle requires costly fodder.
     - **Indivisibility of Goods:** Large assets like horses or cattle cannot be divided into small units to buy small items.
     - **Problem of Deferred Payments:** It was very difficult to lend or borrow because repaying loans in goods caused disputes over changing quality and value.
     - These massive handicaps led human societies to invent money as a universal medium of exchange.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* బార్టర్ విధానంలో డబుల్ కోఇన్సిడెన్స్ లేకపోవడం, వస్తువు విలువను లెక్కించే ఉమ్మడి కొలమానం లేకపోవడం, నిల్వ ఉంచలేకపోవడం, విభజించలేకపోవడం వంటి 5 ప్రధాన లోపాలు ఉండేవి.

2. **Describe the journey of money from barter to digital forms.**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - **Stage 1 (Barter & Commodity Money):** People exchanged items like grain, salt, shells, and livestock.
     - **Stage 2 (Metallic Coins):** Around 600 BCE, punch-marked silver and copper coins were introduced, making value durable, countable, and easy to carry.
     - **Stage 3 (Dynastic Coinage):** Kings minted gold and silver coins with royal emblems, standard weights, and guaranteed purity.
     - **Stage 4 (Paper Currency):** Governments and central banks issued paper notes backed by law, which were light and safe for large business transactions.
     - **Stage 5 (Plastic & Bank Money):** Banks introduced cheques, demand drafts, and plastic debit/credit cards with magnetic chips.
     - **Stage 6 (Digital & UPI Money):** Today, smartphones enable instant cashless payments via UPI, QR codes, and digital wallets across the world.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* వస్తుమార్పిడి -> గవ్వలు, ఉప్పు -> లోహ నాణేలు -> కాగితపు నోట్లు -> ప్లాస్టిక్ కార్డులు -> నేటి స్మార్ట్‌ఫోన్ UPI డిజిటల్ చెల్లింపుల వరకు డబ్బు పరిణామం చెందింది.

3. **Discuss the significance of coinage in ancient India.**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - The introduction of coins around 600 BCE revolutionized trade by eliminating the problems of the barter system.
     - Coins provided a standard, universally accepted measure of value and weight for buying and selling goods.
     - They allowed kings to collect taxes, pay standing armies, and fund large public works like irrigation dams and roads.
     - Coins serve as vital historical records: their inscriptions, royal portraits, dates, and symbols tell historians about rulers, religion, and economic prosperity.
     - For example, Roman coins discovered in South India prove active maritime trade between Tamil kingdoms and Rome.
     - Satavahana ship coins reflect their naval power, while Kushana coins show the religious fusion of Greek, Persian, and Indian deities.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* ప్రాచీన భారతదేశంలో నాణేలు వాణిజ్యాన్ని సులభతరం చేశాయి. రాజులు సైన్యానికి జీతాలు ఇవ్వడానికి, పన్నులు వసూలు చేయడానికి తోడ్పడ్డాయి. నాణేలపై బొమ్మలు ఆనాటి చరిత్రను తెలుపుతాయి.

4. **How does the Jonbeel Mela show barter in modern times?**
   - **Simple Answer:**
     - Jonbeel Mela is a traditional, historic three-day annual fair held in Morigaon district of Assam.
     - It is organized by the Tiwa tribal community and has been celebrated since the 15th century.
     - During the fair, hill tribes (Tiwa, Karbi, Khasi) come down to the plains to meet plain dwellers.
     - They trade their hill products (ginger, turmeric, bamboo items, herbs) for plain products (fish, beaten rice, pithas) without using a single rupee.
     - It shows that barter can still survive as a symbol of cultural harmony, mutual trust, and brotherhood in the modern age.
   - *తెలుగు వివరణ:* అసోంలోని జాన్‌బీల్ మేళాలో 15వ శతాబ్దం నుంచి కొండ జాతుల ప్రజలు, మైదాన ప్రాంత ప్రజలు డబ్బు ఉపయోగించకుండా అల్లం, పసుపును చేపలు, బియ్యంతో పరస్పరం మార్చుకుంటారు.
