Chapter 1: Union Budget 2025 (Page 5)

Context & Core Summary:

The Union Budget presented by the Finance Minister focuses on 4 core pillars: Youth, Women, Farmers (Annadata), and the Poor (Gareeb). Key budgetary allocations prioritize capital expenditure (CapEx) to spur infrastructure, fiscal consolidation to bring down the fiscal deficit below 4.5% of GDP, and positioning agriculture as the primary engine of inclusive economic growth.

Textbook Exercises: Tick (✓) the correct option

Question 1

The total expenditure estimated for the budget year 2025-26 (excluding borrowings) is approximately:
- a. ₹34.96 lakh crore
- b. ₹50.65 lakh crore
- c. ₹28.37 lakh crore
- d. ₹14.82 lakh crore

Target Answer Key: b. ₹50.65 lakh crore

Formal Explanation: The Union Budget for the fiscal year estimates an overall budget expenditure crossing the landmark ₹50 lakh crore threshold, pegged at approximately ₹50.65 lakh crore to balance development spending with fiscal consolidation.

💡 Memory Hack: Remember "Fifty-Fifty" — the Indian Union Budget breaks the 50-lakh-crore barrier at ₹50.65 lakh crore.

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: Do not confuse Total Expenditure (₹50.65 lakh crore) with Net Tax Receipts (₹28.37 lakh crore) or Revenue Expenditure.

✅ Sanity Check: Total expenditure is always the largest figure in the budget equation: $\text{Expenditure} = \text{Revenue Receipts} + \text{Capital Receipts} + \text{Borrowings}$.

Question 2

The fiscal deficit target for the year 2025-26 is set at:
- a. 5.0% of GDP
- b. 4.4% of GDP
- c. 3.1% of GDP
- d. 6.2% of GDP

Target Answer Key: b. 4.4% of GDP

Formal Explanation: The Government of India committed to a strict fiscal consolidation roadmap under the FRBM framework to bring the fiscal deficit below 4.5% by FY 2025-26. Thus, the target is set at 4.4% of Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

💡 Memory Hack: "Below 4.5" — the target is precisely 4.4%.

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: Students often select 5.0% or 5.1% because that was the target for the previous year (2024-25). The 2025-26 target is strictly 4.4%.

✅ Sanity Check: Fiscal deficit trend must show a declining trajectory year-on-year (e.g., $5.8\% \rightarrow 5.1\% \rightarrow 4.4\%$).

Question 3

The estimated capital expenditure (CapEx) for 2025-26 amounts to about:
- a. ₹11.21 lakh crore
- b. ₹8.50 lakh crore
- c. ₹12.75 lakh crore
- d. ₹9.90 lakh crore

Target Answer Key: a. ₹11.21 lakh crore

Formal Explanation: Capital Expenditure (CapEx), which is money spent on creating long-term productive physical assets like national highways, railways, bridges, airports, and power grids, is maintained at an all-time record level of approximately ₹11.21 lakh crore (representing ~3.4% of GDP).

💡 Memory Hack: "11-21" like an auspicious gift amount (Shagun) — ₹11.21 lakh crore.

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: Do not confuse CapEx (₹11.21 lakh crore) with Revenue Expenditure which covers salaries, pensions, and subsidies.

✅ Sanity Check: CapEx should be roughly 22% of total budget expenditure ($\frac{11.21}{50.65} \approx 22.1\%$).

Question 4

Under the Budget, agriculture is referred to as:
- a. The third engine of growth
- b. The second engine of growth
- c. The first engine of development
- d. A minor priority sector

Target Answer Key: c. The first engine of development

Formal Explanation: The Union Budget specifically designates Agriculture and rural prosperity as the "First Engine of Development," emphasizing productivity enhancements, natural farming, pulse self-sufficiency, and Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) for agriculture.

💡 Memory Hack: Farmers come first in India — "First Engine".

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: Do not pick "The second engine of growth" or "third engine". The official speech calls it the First Engine of Development.

Question 5

The estimated net tax receipts (other than borrowings) for 2025-26 are:
- a. ₹34.96 lakh crore
- b. ₹28.37 lakh crore
- c. ₹50.65 lakh crore
- d. ₹14.82 lakh crore

Target Answer Key: b. ₹28.37 lakh crore

Formal Explanation: Net tax receipts represent the net tax revenue flowing into the Consolidated Fund of India after transferring the mandatory devolution share (41%) to the States. For the budget year, net tax receipts are estimated at ₹28.37 lakh crore.

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: Gross Tax Receipts are higher (~₹38.4 lakh crore); Net tax receipts after state transfers are ₹28.37 lakh crore.

✅ Sanity Check: Net tax receipts (₹28.37L Cr) + Non-tax revenue (~₹5.5L Cr) + Capital receipts & Borrowings (~₹16.8L Cr) = Total Expenditure (₹50.65L Cr).

Question 6

The government's nominal GDP growth estimate for 2025-26 is about:
- a. 9.2%
- b. 10.1%
- c. 8.5%
- d. 11.0%

Target Answer Key: b. 10.1% (or d. 11.0% based on standard projection models; 10.1% is the baseline assumption used in the budget arithmetic).

Formal Explanation: Nominal GDP accounts for real GDP growth plus anticipated inflation (GDP deflator). The Union Budget estimates nominal GDP growth at approximately 10.1% to 10.5% for budgetary calculations.

💡 Memory Hack: Double digits beginning with 10 — "10.1%".

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: Real GDP growth is around 6.5%–7.0%. Nominal GDP includes inflation and is therefore 10.1%–11.0%. Always check if the question asks for Real or Nominal.

Question 7

The outstanding liabilities of the central government for March 2031 are aimed to be around what percentage of GDP?
- a. 60%
- b. 56.1%
- c. 50%
- d. 45%

Target Answer Key: c. 50%

Formal Explanation: Under the revised debt-to-GDP roadmap, the central government aims to bring down its outstanding public debt liabilities from over 56.8% to 50% of GDP by the end of March 2031.

💡 Memory Hack: Half of GDP = 50%.

⚠️ Ninja Exam Trap: 56.1% is the current level. The future target for March 2031 is 50%.

Fact Box & Teacher's Note (Page 5)

  • Teacher's Note: Encourage students to relate budget highlights with real-life benefits for society and citizens (e.g., cheaper mobile phones, higher farm productivity, tax relief for middle class, faster train corridors).
  • Core Concept: A budget is an Annual Financial Statement presented under Article 112 of the Constitution of India.

Chapter 2: State Governors of India (Pages 6 & 7)

Constitutional Background:

Under Article 153 of the Constitution of India, there shall be a Governor for each State. The Governor is appointed by the President of India under Article 155 and holds office during the pleasure of the President. The Governor is the constitutional and executive head of the state.

Chapter 3: Port Blair to Sri Vijaya Puram (Page 8)

Complete Historical Narrative:

In September 2024, the Government of India officially renamed Port Blair, the capital city of the Union Territory of Andaman and Nicobar Islands, to Sri Vijaya Puram.
- Symbolism of Sri Vijaya Puram: While 'Port Blair' honored British naval surveyor Archibald Blair, 'Sri Vijaya Puram' symbolizes the victory of India's freedom struggle and honours the ancient maritime legacy of the Chola Empire (Sri Vijaya kingdom).
- Geographical Location: Located in the Bay of Bengal, the Andaman & Nicobar archipelago forms a strategic maritime gateway.
- Historic Milestones:
1. Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose: On 30th December 1943, Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose hoisted the National Tricolour at Port Blair, declaring the Andaman and Nicobar Islands as the first Indian territory liberated from British colonial rule. He named the islands Shaheed and Swaraj.
2. Cellular Jail (Kala Pani): Built by the British between 1896 and 1906, it housed heroic Indian freedom fighters including Vinayak Damodar Savarkar (Veer Savarkar), Batukeshwar Dutt, and Yogendra Shukla in solitary confinement.
3. World War II History: During WWII (1942–1945), Japanese forces occupied the islands and built fortified concrete bunkers near Corbyn's Cove, which remain standing today as historical landmarks.

Textbook Exercises: Fill in the blanks (Page 8)

1. The former name of Sri Vijaya Puram was ____.

Target Answer Key: Port Blair

Explanation: Renamed in September 2024 to shed colonial vestiges.

2. The Cellular Jail in Sri Vijaya Puram is associated with India's ____.

Target Answer Key: freedom struggle

Explanation: Thousands of revolutionary patriots endured solitary torture here during the independence movement.

3. Netaji Subhash Chandra Bose hoisted the Indian tricolour here in ____.

Target Answer Key: 1943 (specifically 30 December 1943)

Explanation: Netaji visited the islands after the Japanese handed over administrative control to the Azad Hind government.

4. This Indian union territory is located in ____.

Target Answer Key: Bay of Bengal

Explanation: The Andaman and Nicobar chain separates the Bay of Bengal to the west from the Andaman Sea to the east.

5. Sri Vijaya Puram is the capital of the _ and _ Islands.

Target Answer Key: Andaman and Nicobar

Explanation: It is the administrative headquarters and sole municipal council of the Andaman and Nicobar Islands Union Territory.

Fact Box & Hint Box Verification (Page 8):

Chapter 4: Robot Teachers in Schools (Page 9)

Technological Overview:

Modern classrooms worldwide are incorporating Social and Educational Robotics to make STEM education and language acquisition engaging, adaptive, and interactive.

Robot Profiles & Functionalities:

  1. Nao Robot:
  2. Form Factor: Small (58 cm tall), bipedal humanoid robot.
  3. Key Capabilities: Capable of walking, talking, recognizing faces, tracking motion, and dancing.

  4. Educational Role: Used worldwide to teach Mathematics, Science, and foreign languages through interactive dialogue and gamified exercises.

  5. Pepper Robot:

  6. Form Factor: Larger humanoid robot (120 cm tall) on a wheeled mobile base.
  7. Key Capabilities: Equipped with high-level emotion recognition software; interprets facial expressions, voice pitch, and body language.

  8. Educational Role: Acts as an interactive school receptionist, answers students' open-ended questions, and delivers empathetic conversational instruction.

  9. Robovie:

  10. Form Factor: Interactive tabletop and mobile robotics platform developed by ATR (Japan).
  11. Key Capabilities: Speech recognition, tactile sensors, interactive displays.

  12. Educational Role: Designed specifically to assist students in mastering computer programming and coding logic, encouraging learners to construct basic algorithms.

  13. Tega Robot:

  14. Form Factor: Cute, fluffy, brightly coloured squash-and-stretch robotic creature developed by MIT Media Lab.
  15. Key Capabilities: Smartphone-powered face display, animated expressive movements, speech synthesis.
  16. Educational Role: Specifically tailored for early childhood education to improve reading, storytelling, and writing literacy.
