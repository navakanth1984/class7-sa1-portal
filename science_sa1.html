Lesson 5: Changes Around Us: Physical and Chemical (Pages 21–36)

🔬 Chapter Synopsis & Core Scientific Principles

  • Physical Change: A change in which only the physical properties (such as shape, size, color, or state of matter) of a substance undergo transformation, with no new substance formed. Most physical changes are reversible (e.g., melting of ice, stretching a rubber band, dissolving sugar in water), though some may be irreversible (e.g., tearing of paper, breaking of glass).
  • Chemical Change (Chemical Reaction): A change in which one or more entirely new substances with different chemical properties are formed. Chemical changes are almost always irreversible and involve energy exchange (absorption or release of heat/light). Indicators include change in color, evolution of a gas, formation of a precipitate, or change in temperature and smell.
  • Crystallisation: The process of cooling a hot, concentrated solution of a substance to obtain pure crystals of the solid substance (e.g., Copper Sulphate crystals). It is a physical change because no new chemical bonds are created; only the physical arrangement of molecules transitions from liquid to solid lattice.
  • Rusting of Iron: A slow chemical reaction occurring when iron is exposed to both oxygen and water/moisture:
    $$\text{Iron (Fe)} + \text{Oxygen (O}_2\text{)} + \text{Water (H}_2\text{O)} \longrightarrow \text{Hydrated Iron Oxide [Rust] (Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O)}$$
    Rusting weakens iron structures, causing immense economic loss. Methods of prevention include applying paint or grease, and galvanisation (depositing a protective layer of zinc metal on iron).
  • Combustion & The Fire Triangle: A chemical process where a combustible substance reacts rapidly with oxygen, releasing heat and light. The three essential conditions (Fire Triangle) are:
  • Fuel (combustible material)
  • Oxygen (supporter of combustion)
  • Heat (reaching ignition temperature)
    Extinguishing a fire involves removing any one of these three components (e.g., spraying water lowers temperature below ignition point; covering with blanket or $CO_2$ cuts off oxygen supply).
  • Burning of Magnesium Ribbon:
    $$2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{MgO} \quad (\text{Magnesium Oxide - white ash})$$
    When dissolved in water: $\text{MgO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow \text{Mg(OH)}_2$ (basic in nature, turns red litmus blue).
  • Simultaneous Physical & Chemical Change (Candle Burning):
  • Physical Change: Melting of solid wax into liquid wax and vaporisation of molten wax by the heat of the flame (reversible state changes).
  • Chemical Change: Burning of wax vapors in oxygen to produce carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), water vapor ($H_2O$), heat, and light (irreversible chemical reaction).

Textbook Exercises — Lesson 5

A. Read each question carefully and tick (✓) the correct option:

Question 1: Which of these is a physical change?
- a. Rusting of iron
- b. Burning of wood
- c. Melting of ice
- d. Curdling of milk

Answer: c. Melting of ice
- Explanation: In melting ice, solid water transforms into liquid water ($H_2O \rightarrow H_2O$). The chemical composition remains completely unchanged, and it can be frozen back into ice.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: మంచు కరగడం అనేది భౌతిక మార్పు (Physical change). ఎందుకంటే ఇందులో కొత్త పదార్థం ఏదీ ఏర్పడదు; కేవలం ఘన రూపంలో ఉన్న నీరు ద్రవ రూపంలోకి మారుతుంది. దీనిని మళ్లీ చల్లబరిస్తే మంచుగా మార్చవచ్చు.
- ⚠️ Exam Trap: Rusting, burning, and curdling all produce entirely new chemical substances with new properties, making them chemical changes.

Question 2: Which of the following occurs during a chemical change?
- a. Change in state only
- b. Formation of one or more new substances
- c. Reversible transformation
- d. Change in shape only

Answer: b. Formation of one or more new substances
- Explanation: The definitive hallmark of a chemical change is the production of new substances with distinct chemical bonds and properties.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: రసాయనిక మార్పు (Chemical change) జరిగినప్పుడు తప్పనిసరిగా ఒకటి లేదా అంతకంటే ఎక్కువ సరికొత్త పదార్థాలు ఏర్పడతాయి.

Question 3: What is the product of combustion when magnesium ribbon is burned in air?
- a. Magnesium chloride
- b. Magnesium oxide
- c. Magnesium sulphate
- d. Magnesium carbonate

Answer: b. Magnesium oxide
- Explanation: Magnesium reacts with atmospheric oxygen to form magnesium oxide white powder: $2Mg + O_2 \rightarrow 2MgO$.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: మెగ్నీషియం తీగను గాలిలో మండించినప్పుడు అది ఆక్సిజన్‌తో చర్య జరిపి తెల్లటి మెగ్నీషియం ఆక్సైడ్ ($MgO$) బూడిదను ఏర్పరుస్తుంది.

Question 4: Which of the following is an essential condition for rusting to take place?
- a. Presence of dry nitrogen
- b. Presence of oxygen and moisture (water)
- c. Complete absence of air
- d. High temperature above $100^\circ\text{C}$

Answer: b. Presence of oxygen and moisture (water)
- Explanation: Rusting of iron cannot occur in dry air alone or in boiled, oxygen-free water. Both oxygen and water vapor are simultaneously required.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: ఇనుము తుప్పు పట్టడానికి గాలిలోని ఆక్సిజన్ మరియు తేమ (నీరు) రెండూ తప్పనిసరిగా ఉండాలి.

Question 5: Galvanisation is the process of coating iron with a protective layer of:
- a. Copper
- b. Aluminium
- c. Zinc
- d. Silver

Answer: c. Zinc
- Explanation: Zinc forms an impermeable sacrificial layer over iron, preventing air and moisture from contacting the underlying iron surface.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: ఇనుము తుప్పు పట్టకుండా ఉండటానికి దానిపై జింక్ (సత్తు) లోహపు పూత పూయడాన్ని 'గాల్వనైజేషన్' (Galvanisation) అంటారు.

E. Descriptive Questions — Short & Long Answers

Question 1: How do physical changes differ from chemical changes? Give two examples of each.
- Answer:
| Feature | Physical Change | Chemical Change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Formation of New Substance | No new substance is formed. | One or more entirely new substances are formed. |
| Nature of Change | Temporary and usually reversible. | Permanent and usually irreversible. |
| Chemical Composition | Chemical properties remain unchanged. | Original chemical properties are completely altered. |
| Energy Changes | Generally little or no heat/light energy is exchanged. | Significant energy is either absorbed or released. |
| Examples | Melting of ice, tearing of paper. | Rusting of iron, burning of wood. |
- తెలుగు వివరణ: భౌతిక మార్పులో ఎటువంటి సరికొత్త పదార్థం ఏర్పడదు (ఉదా: మంచు కరగడం, కాగితం చింపడం). రసాయనిక మార్పులో సరికొత్త లక్షణాలు గల కొత్త పదార్థాలు శాశ్వతంగా ఏర్పడతాయి (ఉదా: ఇనుము తుప్పు పట్టడం, కలప కాలడం).

Question 2: What is rusting? Explain the conditions necessary for rusting of iron and list two methods to prevent it.
- Answer:
1. Definition: Rusting is the slow oxidation of iron in the presence of air and moisture to form a reddish-brown flaky substance called hydrated iron oxide (rust).
2. Chemical Equation:
$$4\text{Fe} + 3\text{O}_2 + 2x\text{H}_2\text{O} \longrightarrow 2\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$$
3. Necessary Conditions: Both oxygen (from air) and water/moisture must be simultaneously present.
4. Prevention Methods:
- Painting or Greasing: Applying a coat of oil, grease, or paint isolates the iron surface from air and moisture.
- Galvanisation: Depositing a thin protective coating of zinc metal onto iron sheets or pipes prevents oxidation.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: గాలిలోని ఆక్సిజన్ మరియు తేమతో ఇనుము చర్య జరిపి ఎరుపు-గోధుమ రంగు తుప్పును ఏర్పరచడాన్ని తుప్పు పట్టడం అంటారు. దీనిని నివారించడానికి రంగులు (పెయింట్) వేయడం, గ్రీజు పూయడం లేదా జింక్ పూత పూసే గాల్వనైజేషన్ పద్ధతులను ఉపయోగిస్తారు.

Question 3: What is the Fire Triangle? Draw and explain the role of each component, and describe how removing any one component extinguishes a fire.
- Answer:
1. Definition: The Fire Triangle is a conceptual model representing the three essential components required for combustion: Fuel, Oxygen, and Heat.
2. Roles of Components:
- Fuel: The combustible material that undergoes burning (e.g., wood, petrol, LPG).
- Oxygen: The chemical supporter that reacts with the fuel to maintain the oxidation process.
- Heat: Energy required to raise the temperature of the fuel to its minimum ignition temperature to start burning.
3. Extinguishing Mechanism:
- Removing Heat: Spraying water absorbs large amounts of heat, bringing the temperature below the fuel's ignition point.
- Removing Oxygen: Smothering with a fire blanket, sand, or spraying carbon dioxide ($CO_2$) cuts off atmospheric oxygen supply.
- Removing Fuel: Turning off the gas valve or creating firebreaks in a forest removes the combustible material.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: దహనం జరగడానికి ఇంధనం, ఆక్సిజన్, మరియు తగినంత ఉష్ణం (ఇగ్నిషన్ ఉష్ణోగ్రత) అనే మూడు అంశాలు అవసరం. వీటిలో ఏ ఒక్కదానిని తొలగించినా మంటలు ఆరిపోతాయి (ఉదా: నీరు చల్లడం ద్వారా ఉష్ణోగ్రతను తగ్గించవచ్చు, ఇసుక లేదా కార్బన్ డైయాక్సైడ్ చల్లడం ద్వారా ఆక్సిజన్ అందకుండా చేయవచ్చు).

Question 4: Is the burning of a candle a physical change, a chemical change, or both? Justify your answer.
- Answer:
The burning of a candle represents both physical and chemical changes occurring simultaneously:
1. Physical Change: The heat of the flame melts the solid wax into liquid wax near the wick, and capillary action draws it up where it vaporises. If the candle is extinguished, the molten wax cools down and solidifies back into wax without changing its chemical identity.
2. Chemical Change: The wax vapors react with oxygen in the air to undergo combustion, producing carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), water vapor ($H_2O$), soot (carbon), heat, and light. This reaction is irreversible and consumes the wax chemically.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: కొవ్వొత్తి వెలగడం అనేది భౌతిక మరియు రసాయనిక మార్పులు రెండూ ఒకే సమయంలో జరగడానికి అద్భుతమైన ఉదాహరణ. మైనం కరిగి ద్రవంగా మారడం భౌతిక మార్పు; మైనపు ఆవిరి ఆక్సిజన్‌తో మండి కార్బన్ డైయాక్సైడ్ మరియు నీటి ఆవిరిగా మారడం రసాయనిక మార్పు.

Question 5: Curdling of milk is considered a chemical change. Justify this statement.
- Answer:
Curdling of milk is a chemical change because:
1. When an acidic substance (like lemon juice) or lactic acid bacteria is introduced, the lactose sugar in milk is converted into lactic acid, and milk proteins (casein) coagulate.
2. A new substance, curd (or paneer), is formed, which possesses completely different chemical properties, taste, and texture from liquid milk.
3. The process is completely irreversible; curd cannot be converted back into original fresh milk.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: పాలు పెరుగుగా మారడం ఒక రసాయనిక మార్పు. ఎందుకంటే బ్యాక్టీరియా చర్య వల్ల పాలలోని లాక్టోస్ లాక్టిక్ ఆమ్లంగా మారి సరికొత్త లక్షణాలు గల పెరుగు ఏర్పడుతుంది. పెరుగును మళ్లీ పాలుగా మార్చలేము.

F. Competency-Based & Analytical Questions

Case Study 1: A gardener collects dry fallen leaves, vegetable peels, and garden waste into a compost pit. Over several weeks, the waste decomposes through the action of microorganisms into dark, nutrient-rich compost.
1. Is the formation of compost a physical or a chemical change? Explain why.
- Answer: It is a chemical change. Microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) break down complex organic compounds into simpler chemical substances, generating heat and releasing gases. The resulting compost has entirely different properties from raw vegetable peels and cannot be reversed.
- తెలుగు: వ్యర్థాలు కుళ్ళి కంపోస్ట్ ఎరువుగా మారడం ఒక రసాయనిక మార్పు.
2. State one condition that accelerates this composting process.
- Answer: Presence of moisture and proper aeration (oxygen), which promotes the aerobic respiration of decomposer microbes.

Lesson 6: Adolescence: A Stage of Growth and Change (Pages 37–56)

🧬 Chapter Synopsis & Core Biological Concepts

  • Adolescence: The transitional period of life between childhood and adulthood (usually from 10–11 years to 18–19 years), during which a young person undergoes rapid physical, physiological, mental, and emotional growth. Adolescents are often referred to as 'teenagers'.
  • Puberty: The specific developmental period within adolescence during which human beings undergo physical and reproductive maturation, becoming biologically capable of reproduction.
  • Physical Changes During Puberty:
  • Growth Spurt: Rapid increase in the length of long bones (arms and legs), leading to a sudden surge in height.
  • Body Structure: In boys, shoulders and chest broaden, and muscles develop more prominently. In girls, the pelvic and hip region widens, and body fat deposits increase around the hips and thighs.
  • Voice Changes: In boys, the larynx (voice box) grows significantly, protruding outwards in the throat as Adam's apple, resulting in a deep, cracking voice. In girls, the larynx remains smaller, producing a high-pitched voice.
  • Sweat & Sebaceous Glands: Increased activity of sweat and oil (sebaceous) glands often leads to acne and pimples on the face.
  • Secondary Sexual Characteristics:
    • Boys: Facial hair (mustache and beard), hair on chest, underarms, and pubic area; enlargement of testes and penis; production of sperm.
    • Girls: Enlargement of breasts and mammary glands; hair in underarms and pubic area; maturation of ovaries and release of mature ova (eggs).
  • Hormones & Endocrine System:
  • Hormones are chemical messengers secreted directly into the bloodstream by ductless (endocrine) glands.
  • Pituitary Gland: Located at the base of the brain; known as the Master Gland because its hormones stimulate the testes and ovaries to secrete their respective sex hormones.
  • Testosterone: Male sex hormone secreted by testes; regulates sperm development and male secondary sexual characteristics.
  • Oestrogen & Progesterone: Female sex hormones secreted by ovaries; regulate ovum development, female secondary sexual characteristics, and the menstrual cycle.
  • Other Vital Endocrine Glands:
    • Thyroid Gland: Secretes Thyroxine (requires iodine; deficiency causes Goitre).
    • Pancreas: Secretes Insulin (regulates blood glucose; deficiency causes Diabetes).
    • Adrenal Glands: Secrete Adrenaline (the 'fight or flight' emergency hormone that manages heart rate, blood pressure, and stress).
  • The Menstrual Cycle in Females:
  • Begins with Menarche (the first occurrence of menstruation, around age 11–13) and ends around age 45–50 with Menopause.
  • Occurs roughly every 28 to 30 days:
    1. An ovary releases one mature ovum (ovulation).
    2. The uterine wall thickens with blood vessels to prepare for a fertilised egg.
    3. If fertilisation does not occur, the egg and the thickened uterine lining break down and are discharged through the vagina as blood and tissue (menstruation), lasting 3 to 7 days.
  • Nutritional Needs of Adolescents:
  • Adolescents require a well-balanced diet rich in Proteins (for rapid muscle and tissue growth), Carbohydrates and Fats (for energy), Vitamins and Minerals (Iron for hemoglobin in blood; Calcium and Phosphorus for bones and teeth).
  • Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyan (Say NO to Drugs):
  • Drugs and alcohol cause severe physical, emotional, and social damage. They impair brain development, cause addiction, and ruin academic and career prospects.
  • Injecting drugs with shared syringes carries extreme risk of fatal viral infections like HIV/AIDS and Hepatitis B.

Question 3: What is the menstrual cycle? Explain the key biological events that occur during this cycle.
- Answer:
1. Overview: The menstrual cycle is a natural, recurring monthly cycle in adolescent and adult females governed by pituitary and ovarian hormones (oestrogen and progesterone).
2. Sequence of Events (approximately 28–30 days):
- Phase 1 (Follicular / Proliferative): One ovum matures inside an ovary, and the inner lining of the uterus (endometrium) thickens and becomes rich with blood vessels to prepare for receiving a fertilised egg.
- Phase 2 (Ovulation): Around day 14 of the cycle, the mature ovum is released from the ovary into the fallopian tube.
- Phase 3 (Luteal / Menstruation): If fertilisation does not occur, the released ovum breaks down. The thickened uterine lining is shed along with blood, mucus, and tissue fluids through the vagina. This bleeding is called menstruation and lasts 3 to 7 days.
- తెలుగు వివరణ: స్త్రీలలో ప్రతి 28-30 రోజులకు ఒక పరిపక్వ అండం విడుదలవుతుంది. గర్భధారణ కోసం గర్భాశయ గోడలు రక్తనాళాలతో చిక్కగా మారతాయి. ఫలదీకరణం జరగనప్పుడు ఆ గోడలు విచ్ఛిన్నమై రక్తం రూపంలో బయటకు విడుదలవుతాయి. దీనినే ఋతుచక్రం అంటారు.

- Answer:
