Lesson 5: The Adventure of the Mason (Pages 58–66)

Author: Washington Irving (1783–1859)

📖 Story Synopsis:

  • English Summary: In the ancient Spanish city of Granada, an honest but impoverished mason struggles to feed his family. One night, a tall, pale stranger knocks on his door and hires him for a secret task on the condition that he be blindfolded. Led through twisting alleys into a grand courtyard with an old Moorish fountain, the mason builds a concealed brick vault. Before sealing it, the stranger has him place two heavy sacks into the vault; to the mason's touch, the sacks feel not like corpses, but like heavy coins or gold. Paid generously with gold coins, he is guided back blindfolded and warned never to remove the blindfold until he reaches home. Years later, an elderly, tight-fisted landlord complains that his abandoned house is considered unlucky and haunted by the sound of clinking coins after an old miser died there without an heir. Recognizing the Moorish fountain in the courtyard as the exact site of his midnight work, the mason cunningly offers to live there rent-free in exchange for repairing the dilapidated property. The landlord agrees. The mason moves in, quietly retrieves the hidden gold from behind the vault, repairs the house, becomes one of the wealthiest and most respected citizens of Granada, and takes the secret of the hidden vault to his grave.
  • తెలుగు సారాంశం (Telugu Summary): గ్రనడా నగరంలో నిరుపేద రాతి మేస్త్రీ ఒకడు ఉండేవాడు. ఒక అర్ధరాత్రి ఒక విచిత్రమైన ఆగంతకుడు వచ్చి కళ్లకు గంతలు కట్టి ఒక పాత భవనంలోని నీళ్ల ఫౌంటెన్ కింద రహస్య గదిని నిర్మించమని చెప్పాడు. మేస్త్రీతో రెండు బరువైన సంచులను ఆ గదిలో పెట్టించి గోడ కట్టించాడు. ఆ సంచులను తాకినప్పుడు అందులో శవాలు కాక బంగారు నాణేలు ఉన్నాయని మేస్త్రీ గ్రహించాడు. మేస్త్రీకి బంగారు నాణేలు కూలీగా ఇచ్చి తిరిగి గంతలు కట్టి పంపించారు. కొన్నేళ్ల తర్వాత ఒక పిసినారి యజమాని తన పాడుబడ్డ ఇంట్లో అర్ధరాత్రి నాణేల చప్పుడు వస్తోందని, ఎవరూ అద్దెకు రావడం లేదని వాపోయాడు. ఆ ఇంటిని చూడగానే గతంలో తాను గోడ కట్టిన ప్రదేశమేనని మేస్త్రీ గుర్తించాడు. ఉచితంగా ఇల్లు బాగుచేసి ఉంటానని యజమానితో ఒప్పందం కుదుర్చుకుని, లోపల ఉన్న రహస్య ధనాన్ని తీసి అత్యంత ధనవంతుడయ్యాడు. ఆ రహస్యాన్ని ఎవరికీ చెప్పకుండా తనతోనే సమాధి చేసుకున్నాడు.

D. Reference to Context (RTC) (Page 63):

1. "I have heard that you are an honest man. I am in need of someone I can trust."
- a. Who said this line and to whom?

Answer: The tall, pale stranger said this line to the poor mason standing at his door late at night.
- b. Why do you think the stranger chose the mason?
Answer: The stranger needed an honest, trustworthy craftsman who would carry out a top-secret construction task without prying, asking suspicious questions, or revealing the hidden vault to anyone.
- c. What does this tell us about the mason's character?
Answer: It proves that the mason enjoyed an unblemished reputation for integrity, honesty, and professional discretion throughout Granada.

2. "Let me live in this house rent-free until a proper tenant comes along."
- a. Why did the mason make this request?

Answer: The mason recognized the Moorish fountain in the dilapidated courtyard and realized this was the very house where he had sealed the gold coins in the vault. Living there rent-free gave him uninterrupted access to recover the treasure.
- b. What did the mason plan to do in return?
Answer: He promised to repair the crumbling walls, replace broken roof tiles, and restore the abandoned property to fit living condition.
- c. How did this decision affect his future?
Answer: It transformed his life completely; by secretly retrieving the hoard of gold coins, he escaped lifelong poverty, educated and supported his family, and became one of the wealthiest and most esteemed men in Granada.

E. Answer the following questions in detail (Page 63):

Q1: Why did the stranger blindfold the mason before taking him to the secret place?

Answer: The stranger blindfolded the mason so that he could never discover the geographical location or route to the hidden property in Granada, thereby keeping the concealed vault and its treasure absolutely secure from theft or exposure.

Q2: How did the mason feel when he touched the sacks that looked like bodies?

Answer: The mason was initially terrified because he was told he was carrying corpses. However, upon lifting them, he noticed the unusual metallic weight and clinking feel, realizing they were actually heavy sacks loaded with gold coins rather than human bodies.

Q3: What made the mason believe that the old house was the same place he had visited before?

Answer: When the landlord walked him into the dusty, cobweb-covered courtyard, the mason saw the unique, dry Moorish fountain in the center, identical in shape, carving, and surrounding layout to the hall he had worked in years earlier.

Q4: How did the mason's life change after he moved into the old house?

Answer: His fortunes soared. While diligently repairing the house brick-by-brick, he accessed the secret vault and retrieved the gold coins. He bought neat clothes, provided nutritious food and education for his children, and lived in comfort and high social respect.

Q5: What moral does the story of the mason teach us?

Answer: The story teaches that honesty, patient diligence, and quiet discretion (the wisdom of knowing when to remain silent) yield far greater and more enduring rewards than greed and boastfulness.

F. Think and Answer (Page 63):

  1. Why do you think the mason never revealed the secret of the hidden vault even to his own family? Do you believe silence can sometimes be wiser than sharing everything?

    Answer: The mason kept silent because disclosing the vault could invite greed, legal disputes with the landlord, or public suspicion of stolen property. Yes, discreet silence is often wiser than loose talk because it protects one's family and preserves hard-earned peace.

  2. The mason became rich but stayed honest and hardworking. Do you think a person's character should change when they gain wealth or success?

    Answer: No, wealth should never corrupt one's moral values. The mason set an inspiring example by continuing his honest labour and using his wealth charitably without arrogance.

Vocabulary: Compound Words (Pages 64–65)

  • Definition: A compound word is formed when two separate words join together to create a single new word with a distinct meaning.
  • Three Types of Compound Words:
  • Closed Compound Words: Written as a single combined word without spaces. (Examples: notebook, classroom, sunlight, passageway, courtyard, blindfold, deathbed).
  • Hyphenated Compound Words: Joined together by a hyphen (-). (Examples: mother-in-law, well-known, runner-up, lantern-light, moon-lit, run-down).
  • Open Compound Words: Written as two separate words that together function as a single conceptual unit. (Examples: post office, high school, ice cream, gold coins, stone path, brick wall).

Grammar Time: Active Voice and Passive Voice (Page 65)

  • Core Rule & Formula:
  • Active Voice: The subject performs the action. $\text{Subject} + \text{Verb} + \text{Object}$.
  • Passive Voice: The object becomes the subject and receives the action. $\text{Object} + \text{Helping Verb (be)} + \text{Past Participle (V3)} + \text{by} + \text{Agent}$.

J. Rewrite the Active Voice sentences in the Passive Voice:

  1. The stranger blindfolded the mason.

    Passive: The mason was blindfolded by the stranger.

  2. The mason built a wall inside the courtyard.

    Passive: A wall was built inside the courtyard by the mason.

  3. The man gave the mason a bag of gold.

    Passive: The mason was given a bag of gold by the man. (or: A bag of gold was given to the mason by the man).

  4. The mason repaired the old fountain.

    Passive: The old fountain was repaired by the mason.

  5. The stranger sealed the vault carefully.

    Passive: The vault was sealed carefully by the stranger.

  6. The landlord offered him a job.

    Passive: He was offered a job by the landlord. (or: A job was offered to him by the landlord).

  7. The mason restored the broken house.

    Passive: The broken house was restored by the mason.

  8. The city people admired the mason's honesty.

    Passive: The mason's honesty was admired by the city people.

Lesson 6: The Train Saviours (Pages 67–78)

Author: E. Nesbit (Edith Nesbit, 1858–1924) — adapted from The Railway Children

📖 Story Synopsis:

  • English Summary: Three siblings—Bobbie (Roberta), Peter, and Phyllis—live near a railway line in the countryside. While on a walk towards the railway tunnel, they hear a sinister rustling sound on the steep wooded slope above the tracks. Suddenly, an enormous landslide occurs: rocks, loose earth, and large trees crash down, completely burying the railway line. Knowing that the 11:29 train is due in minutes and that running to the distant station in time is impossible, Peter devises a brilliant plan. Remembering that red signifies danger, they persuade the girls to take off their bright red flannel petticoats. Peter cuts them into six flags and fixes them onto wooden broomsticks and branches. Stationing themselves along the curve before the tunnel, the children wave their red flags frantically as the heavy express train roars towards them. Bobbie runs onto the tracks, waving her flag until the last second. The driver spots the red warning signals, engages the emergency brakes, and brings the train to a screeching halt just twenty yards before the heap of trees and rubble. The passengers and driver hail the brave children as heroes who saved hundreds of lives.
  • తెలుగు సారాంశం (Telugu Summary): బాబీ, పీటర్, మరియు ఫిలిస్ అనే ముగ్గురు పిల్లలు రైల్వే లైన్ సమీపంలో నివసిస్తుంటారు. ఒకరోజు వారు నడుస్తుండగా కొండచరియలు విరిగిపడి పెద్ద చెట్లు, రాళ్లతో రైల్వే ట్రాక్ పూర్తిగా మూసుకుపోతుంది. కొద్ది నిమిషాల్లో ఎక్స్‌ప్రెస్ రైలు రాబోతోంది. స్టేషన్‌కు వెళ్లి చెప్పే సమయం లేదు. ఎరుపు రంగు ప్రమాదానికి సంకేతం అని గుర్తుచేసుకున్న పీటర్, బాలికల ఎరుపు రంగు పావడాలను (పెటికోట్స్) ముక్కలుగా కోసి ఆరు జెండాలు తయారు చేశాడు. రైలు వేగంగా వస్తుండగా ట్రాక్ పక్కన నిలబడి ఆ జెండాలను ఊపారు. బాబీ ధైర్యంతో ట్రాక్ పై నిలబడి జెండా ఊపడంతో డ్రైవర్ ప్రమాదాన్ని గుర్తించి ఎమర్జెన్సీ బ్రేకులు వేసి రైలును ఆపాడు. వందలాది ప్రయాణికుల ప్రాణాలను కాపాడిన ఆ పిల్లలను అందరూ హీరోలుగా కొనియాడారు.

Grammar Time: Direct and Indirect Speech (Pages 74–75)

  • Rules for Changing Direct into Indirect (Reported) Speech:
  • Remove inverted commas ("...") and insert the conjunction that (for statements).
  • Shift tenses one step backward when the reporting verb is in the past:
    • Present Simple $\rightarrow$ Past Simple (is/am $\rightarrow$ was, are $\rightarrow$ were, go $\rightarrow$ went).
    • Present Continuous $\rightarrow$ Past Continuous (is running $\rightarrow$ was running).
    • Present Perfect $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect (has seen $\rightarrow$ had seen).
    • Simple Past $\rightarrow$ Past Perfect (saw $\rightarrow$ had seen).
  • Change pronouns according to the speaker and listener.
  • Time/Place changes: now $\rightarrow$ then, today $\rightarrow$ that day, here $\rightarrow$ there, tomorrow $\rightarrow$ the next day.

Exercises: Change into Indirect Speech:

  1. Peter said, "The train is coming."

    $\rightarrow$ Peter said that the train was coming.

  2. Bobbie said, "I hear a strange sound in the bushes."

    $\rightarrow$ Bobbie said that she heard a strange sound in the bushes.

  3. Phyllis said, "We must stop the train."

    $\rightarrow$ Phyllis said that they had to stop the train.

  4. The driver said, "You children have saved hundreds of lives."

    $\rightarrow$ The driver said that those children had saved hundreds of lives.

  5. The children said, "We made flags from our red petticoats."

    $\rightarrow$ The children said that they had made flags from their red petticoats.

Poem: Past and Future (Pages 79–83)

Poet: Elizabeth Barrett Browning (1806–1861)

📖 Stanza-by-Stanza Analysis & Theme:

  • Core Meaning: The poem reflects upon the passage of human time—how the past, filled with sorrow, grief, and dead memories, must yield to the radiant promise of the future.
  • Poetic Devices:
  • Metaphor: The past is compared to an old, austere hermit residing in a dark, quiet mountain cell. In contrast, the future is depicted as a radiant, veiled bride entering a festive banquet hall with joy and anticipation.
  • Rhyme Scheme: Petrarcan Sonnet structure (ABBA ABBA CDCDCD).
  • తెలుగు భావార్థము: గతంలో జరిగిన బాధలను, శోకాలను ముసలి సన్యాసి వలె గుండె లోతుల్లో విడిచిపెట్టి, నూతన వధువులా ఉజ్వలంగా ఆహ్వానించే భవిష్యత్తు వైపు ఆశావహ దృక్పథంతో అడుగులు వేయాలని కవయిత్రి ప్రబోధిస్తున్నారు.

Textbook Questions & Answers (Pages 81–82):

  1. What does the poem compare the past to?

    Answer: An old hermit sitting peacefully in a solitary mountain cave/cell.

  2. What does the poet compare the future to?

    Answer: A youthful, hopeful bride bringing fresh happiness and new beginnings.

  3. What is the central message of the poem?

    Answer: We should let go of past griefs, regrets, and disappointments, and embrace the future with optimism, vitality, and renewed faith.

Lesson 7: The Happy Prince (Pages 84–93)

Author: Oscar Wilde (1854–1900)

📖 Story Synopsis:

  • English Summary: High above a European city stands the majestic, gilded statue of the Happy Prince, adorned with leaves of fine gold, two bright sapphires for eyes, and a rich red ruby glowing on his sword-hilt. In life, the Prince lived in the Palace of Sans-Souci where sorrow was forbidden. But now, raised on a tall column, he witnesses the misery, poverty, and disease of the common people and weeps bitterly. A little Swallow, en route to warm Egypt for the winter, stops between the Prince's feet to rest. Moved by the Prince's compassion, the Swallow agrees to stay night after night to act as his messenger. At the Prince's bidding, the bird plucks the ruby for a poor, exhausted seamstress nursing her feverish son, extracts one sapphire for a starving young playwright freezing in an attic, gives the other sapphire to a weeping little match-girl who dropped her matches in the gutter, and strips off the fine gold leaf-by-leaf to feed starving street children. Completely blind and stripped of his former splendor, the Prince is now accompanied by the Swallow, who refuses to abandon him even as winter sets in. Frozen by the bitter frost, the devoted bird kisses the Prince and drops dead at his feet; at that exact moment, the Prince's leaden heart snaps into two pieces. The next day, the vain Town Councillors pull down the dull, shabby statue and melt it in a furnace, throwing the lead heart onto a dustheap beside the dead bird. In heaven, God commands His angels to bring Him the two most precious things in the city; the angel returns with the broken leaden heart and the dead swallow. God declares: "You have chosen well, for in my garden of Paradise this little bird shall sing for evermore, and in my city of gold the Happy Prince shall praise me."
  • తెలుగు సారాంశం (Telugu Summary): ఒక నగరం మధ్యలో ఆనంద యువరాజు (హ్యాపీ ప్రిన్స్) విగ్రహం ఉంటుంది. దానికి బంగారు పూత, కళ్లకు నీలాలు, ఖడ్గానికి కెంపు పొదిగి ఉంటాయి. బతికున్నప్పుడు అంతఃపురంలో ఆనందంగా గడిపిన యువరాజు, విగ్రహమైన తర్వాత ప్రజల కష్టాలను చూసి కన్నీరు కారుస్తాడు. ఈజిప్టుకు వెళ్తున్న ఒక చిన్న పిట్ట (స్వాల్లో) యువరాజుకు సహాయకుడిగా మారుతుంది. యువరాజు ఆదేశంతో ఆ పిట్ట కెంపును నిరుపేద దర్జీ స్త్రీకి, కళ్లలోని నీలాలను పేద రచయితకు మరియు అగ్గిపెట్టెలమ్మే చిన్నారికి అందిస్తుంది. ఒంటిపై ఉన్న బంగారాన్ని ఆకలితో అలమటించే పేద పిల్లలకు పంచిపెడుతుంది. పూర్తిగా గుడ్డివాడైపోయిన యువరాజును విడిచివెళ్లలేక ఆ పిట్ట చలికి అక్కడే మరణిస్తుంది. ఆ బాధతో విగ్రహంలోని సీసపు గుండె పగిలిపోతుంది. నగరం మేయర్ ఆ విగ్రహాన్ని కరిగించి, పిట్టను, పగిలిన గుండెను చెత్తకుప్పపై పారేస్తారు. దేవుడు దేవదూతను నగరంలోని అత్యంత పవిత్రమైన రెండు వస్తువులు తెమ్మన్నప్పుడు, దేవదూత ఆ పగిలిన గుండెను, మరణించిన పిట్టను స్వర్గానికి తీసుకెళ్తాడు.

Vocabulary: Phrasal Verbs & Register (Pages 91–93)

J. Phrasal Verbs in Context (Page 91):

  1. give away: To donate freely without expecting return. (e.g., The Prince decided to give away all his golden leaves).
  2. throw away: To discard as useless. (e.g., The workmen threw away the lead heart on the dustheap).
  3. fly away: To depart by flying. (e.g., The swallow could not fly away because he loved the Prince).
  4. look after: To care for someone. (e.g., The mother tried to look after her sick child).
  5. break down: To collapse emotionally or shatter. (e.g., The lead heart broke down into two).

H. Formal vs. Informal Language Register (Page 92):

  • Informal: "Can you give this to the boy?" $\rightarrow$ Formal: "Could you please deliver this to the young gentleman?"
  • Informal: "Do you want me to help you with that?" $\rightarrow$ Formal: "May I assist you with that task?"
  • Informal: "I'm telling you, it's really bad." $\rightarrow$ Formal: "I assure you that the situation is exceedingly grave."
