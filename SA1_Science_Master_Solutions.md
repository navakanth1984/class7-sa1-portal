# CHAPTER 5: Changes Around Us: Physical and Chemical (Pages 63–79)

## 🔬 Chapter Synopsis & Core Scientific Principles
1. **Physical Change:** A change in which only the physical properties (shape, size, state, appearance) of a substance undergo a change, and **no new substance is formed**. Most physical changes are reversible (e.g., melting of ice, stretching a rubber band, boiling water), though some may be irreversible in shape (e.g., tearing of paper, breaking of glass).
2. **Chemical Change (Chemical Reaction):** A change in which one or more **new substances with entirely different chemical properties** are formed. Chemical changes are permanent and irreversible (e.g., burning of wood, rusting of iron, curdling of milk, digestion of food). Indicators: evolution of gas, change in colour, release/absorption of heat/light, precipitate formation.
3. **Rusting of Iron:** A slow chemical reaction where iron reacts with oxygen and moisture from the air to form hydrated iron(III) oxide (rust):
   $$\text{Iron (Fe)} + \text{Oxygen (O}_2\text{)} + \text{Water (H}_2\text{O)} \longrightarrow \text{Hydrated Iron Oxide [Rust] (Fe}_2\text{O}_3 \cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O)}$$
   - *Prevention:* Painting, applying grease/oil, galvanisation (coating with zinc), and alloying (stainless steel).
4. **Combustion & The Fire Triangle:** A chemical process where a substance reacts rapidly with oxygen, releasing heat and light.
   - *Three requirements (Fire Triangle):* 1. Fuel, 2. Oxygen (Air), 3. Heat (Ignition Temperature).
   - Burning of magnesium ribbon: $2\text{Mg} + \text{O}_2 \longrightarrow 2\text{MgO}$ (dazzling white light, basic white ash).
5. **Crystallisation:** The process of obtaining pure crystals of a substance from its saturated solution upon cooling or controlled evaporation (e.g., salt from seawater, copper sulphate crystals). It is a **physical change**.
6. **Simultaneous Changes (Candle Burning):**
   - *Physical Change:* Melting and vaporisation of solid wax.
   - *Chemical Change:* Combustion of wax vapour with oxygen producing $CO_2$, water vapour, heat, and light.

**Question 1:** Which of these is a physical change?
- a. Cooking food
- b. Rusting of iron
- c. Boiling water
- d. Burning wood
> **Answer:** **c. Boiling water**
> - **Scientific Reason:** Boiling transforms liquid water into steam (water vapour: $H_2O_{(l)} \rightarrow H_2O_{(g)}$). The chemical identity remains identical, and steam condenses back to water upon cooling. No new chemical substance is formed.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** నీటిని మరిగించడం (Boiling water) అనేది భౌతిక మార్పు. ఇందులో నీరు ఆవిరిగా మారినప్పటికీ రసాయనిక నిర్మాణం మారదు; ఆవిరిని చల్లబరిస్తే తిరిగి నీరుగా మారుతుంది.

**Question 2:** Which of the following occurs during a chemical change?
- a. Melting of ice
- b. Breaking of glass
- c. Formation of a new substance
- d. Tearing paper
> **Answer:** **c. Formation of a new substance**
> - **Scientific Reason:** The fundamental characteristic of a chemical change is the reorganization of atoms to produce new chemical substances with novel properties.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** రసాయనిక మార్పు జరిగినప్పుడు తప్పనిసరిగా సరికొత్త పదార్థం (Formation of a new substance) ఏర్పడుతుంది.

**Question 3:** What is the product of combustion?
- a. Water
- b. Ash and smoke
- c. Oxygen
- d. Ice
> **Answer:** **b. Ash and smoke**
> - **Scientific Reason:** When a carbonaceous fuel burns completely or incompletely, solid mineral residues (ash) and unburnt carbon particles suspended in gases (smoke, $CO_2$) are released.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** దహన చర్య (Combustion) జరిగినప్పుడు బూడిద మరియు పొగ (Ash and smoke) ఉత్పన్నమవుతాయి.

**Question 4:** Which process is an example of crystallisation?
- a. Freezing of water
- b. Boiling of water
- c. Formation of salt crystals from seawater
- d. Cutting paper
> **Answer:** **c. Formation of salt crystals from seawater**
> - **Scientific Reason:** Crystallisation occurs when a dissolved solid separates from a saturated solution as geometric crystal lattices upon slow evaporation or cooling.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** సముద్రపు నీటి నుండి ఉప్పు స్పటికాలు ఏర్పడటం (Formation of salt crystals from seawater) స్పటికీకరణకు (Crystallisation) ఉదాహరణ.

**Question 5:** What happens when iron rusts?
- a. It turns into water
- b. It turns into a new substance
- c. It melts
- d. It changes shape
> **Answer:** **b. It turns into a new substance**
> - **Scientific Reason:** Iron reacts chemically with oxygen and moisture to produce reddish-brown hydrated ferric oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$), which has completely different chemical properties from elemental iron.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** ఇనుము తుప్పు పట్టినప్పుడు అది 'హైడ్రేటెడ్ ఫెర్రిక్ ఆక్సైడ్' అనే సరికొత్త పదార్థంగా (new substance) మారుతుంది.

#### Question 2:
**The experiments shown in Fig. a, b, c and d were performed. Find out in which case(s) did lime water turn milky and why?**
- *(a) Vinegar and baking soda + Lime water*
- *(b) Lemon juice and vinegar + Lime water*
- *(c) Vinegar and common salt + Lime water*
- *(d) Lemon juice and baking soda + Lime water*

> **Answer:** **Lime water turns milky in Case (a) and Case (d).**
> - **Comprehensive Scientific Explanation:**
>   1. **Case (a) [Vinegar + Baking Soda]:** Vinegar contains acetic acid ($CH_3COOH$) and baking soda is sodium hydrogen carbonate ($NaHCO_3$). When they react, brisk effervescence occurs and **Carbon Dioxide gas ($CO_2$)** is released:
>      $$\text{CH}_3\text{COOH} + \text{NaHCO}_3 \longrightarrow \text{CH}_3\text{COONa} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 \uparrow$$
>   2. **Case (d) [Lemon Juice + Baking Soda]:** Lemon juice contains citric acid ($C_6H_8O_7$). Reacting with sodium hydrogen carbonate also releases **Carbon Dioxide gas ($CO_2$)**.
>   3. **Why Lime Water Turns Milky:** When the liberated carbon dioxide gas is bubbled into freshly prepared lime water [Calcium Hydroxide, $Ca(OH)_2$], it forms an insoluble white precipitate of **Calcium Carbonate ($CaCO_3$)**:
>      $$\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{CO}_2 \longrightarrow \text{CaCO}_3 \downarrow \text{ (White Insoluble Precipitate)} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$$
>   4. **Why not in (b) and (c)?**
>      - In Case (b) [Lemon juice + Vinegar]: Both are acids; no carbonate is present, so no $CO_2$ is produced.
>      - In Case (c) [Vinegar + Common Salt]: Acetic acid does not react with neutral $NaCl$ to release $CO_2$.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:**
>   - **కేస్ (a) మరియు కేస్ (d)** లలో మాత్రమే సున్నపు నీరు తెల్లగా పాలవలె మారుతుంది.
>   - ఎందుకంటే వెనిగర్ (ఎసిటిక్ ఆమ్లం) లేదా నిమ్మరసం (సిట్రిక్ ఆమ్లం) వంటసోడా (బేకింగ్ సోడా) తో చర్య జరిపినప్పుడు **కార్బన్ డయాక్సైడ్ ($CO_2$) వాయువు** విడుదలవుతుంది.
>   - ఈ $CO_2$ వాయువు సున్నపు నీటితో ($Ca(OH)_2$) కలిసినప్పుడు కరగని తెల్లటి కాల్షియం కార్బోనేట్ ($CaCO_3$) ఏర్పడటం వలన సున్నపు నీరు పాలవలె తెల్లగా మారుతుంది.

**Q1. How does oxygen contribute to combustion?**
> **Answer:** Oxygen is needed for burning. It helps the fuel catch fire and keeps the flame burning.

> **Answer:** Rusting occurs when iron reacts with both oxygen (air) and moisture (water) to form a brown powdery substance called rust.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** ఇనుము గాలిలోని ఆక్సిజన్ మరియు తేమతో చర్య జరిపినప్పుడు గోధుమ రంగు తుప్పు ఏర్పడుతుంది.

**Q4. Are the changes of water to ice and water to steam, physical or chemical? Explain. [NCERT]**
> **Answer:** Both are **physical changes** because only the form changes (liquid to solid or gas). No new substance is formed, and ice can melt back into water.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:** నీరు మంచుగా లేదా ఆవిరిగా మారడం **భౌతిక మార్పు**. ఎందుకంటే ఇందులో కొత్త పదార్థం ఏదీ ఏర్పడదు; మంచును వేడి చేస్తే మళ్లీ నీరు వస్తుంది.

> **Answer:** Curdling of milk is a **chemical change** because a new substance (curd) with a sour taste is formed, and curd cannot be turned back into milk.

### Section B: Answer the following questions briefly in three to four sentences (Kid-Friendly & Exam-Ready):

**Q1. How do physical changes differ from chemical changes?**
> **Answer:**
> 1. In a **physical change**, no new substance is formed (e.g., melting of ice or tearing paper).
> 2. It only changes the shape, size, or state of matter, and it is usually reversible.
> 3. In a **chemical change**, an entirely new substance is formed with new properties (e.g., burning wood or cooking food).
> 4. Chemical changes are permanent and cannot be reversed easily.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:**
>   - **భౌతిక మార్పు:** కొత్త పదార్థం ఏదీ ఏర్పడదు (ఉదా: మంచు కరగడం). ఇది తాత్కాలికమైనది మరియు వెనక్కి తిప్పవచ్చు.
>   - **రసాయనిక మార్పు:** సరికొత్త పదార్థం ఏర్పడుతుంది (ఉదా: కలప మండడం). ఇది శాశ్వతమైనది మరియు వెనక్కి మార్చలేము.

**Q2. What are the signs that a chemical change has occurred?**
> **Answer:**
> We can tell a chemical change has happened by observing these simple signs:
> 1. **Gas bubbles or fizzing** are produced (e.g., baking soda mixed with vinegar).
> 2. **Change in colour** takes place (e.g., an iron nail turning reddish-brown).
> 3. **Heat or light** is given off or taken in (e.g., a matchstick burning).
> 4. A **new smell** appears or an insoluble solid (precipitate) is formed.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:**
>   - రసాయనిక మార్పు జరిగినప్పుడు వాయువు బుడగలు రావడం, రంగు మారడం, ఉష్ణం లేదా వెలుగు రావడం మరియు కొత్త వాసన రావడం వంటి గుర్తులు కనిపిస్తాయి.

**Q3. What role does heat play in combustion?**
> **Answer:**
> 1. Heat warms up the fuel to its **ignition temperature** (the minimum heat needed to catch fire).
> 2. Without heat, a substance cannot start burning, even if air is present.
> 3. Once burning begins, the reaction produces its own heat to keep the fire going.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:**
>   - ఏదైనా పదార్థం మండాలంటే దానికి తగినంత వేడి (జ్వలన ఉష్ణోగ్రత) అవసరం. వేడి లేకుండా నిప్పు రాజుకోదు. మండడం మొదలయ్యాక అది స్వయంగా వేడిని విడుదల చేస్తూ మండుతుంది.

**Q4. How does crystallisation occur in nature or industry?**
> **Answer:**
> 1. **In Nature:** Seawater collects in shallow pits, and when the sun evaporates the water, pure common salt crystals are left behind.
> 2. **In Industry:** Sugar mills cool down hot, concentrated sugarcane syrup to form clean sugar crystals.
> 3. It is also used in science labs to obtain pure crystals of copper sulphate.
> - **తెలుగు వివరణ:**
>   - ప్రకృతిలో సముద్రపు నీరు ఎండకు ఆవిరైనప్పుడు ఉప్పు స్పటికాలు ఏర్పడతాయి. కర్మాగారాలలో వేడి చెరుకు రసాన్ని చల్లబరిచి స్వచ్ఛమైన పంచదార స్పటికాలను తయారుచేస్తారు.

**Q5. Natural factors, such as wind, rain, etc., help in the formation of soil from rocks. Is this change physical or chemical and why? [NCERT]**
> **Answer:**
> Soil formation involves **both physical and chemical changes**:
> 1. **Physical change:** Strong winds and rushing rainwater break large rocks into small sand grains without changing what they are made of.
> 2. **Chemical change:** Rainwater and acids in the air react with minerals inside the rocks, breaking them down into new clay and rich soil minerals.

# CHAPTER 6: Adolescence: A Stage of Growth and Change (Pages 80–95)

## 🔬 Chapter Synopsis & Core Scientific Principles
1. **Adolescence & Puberty:**
   - **Adolescence:** The transitional period of human growth and development between childhood and adulthood, typically spanning from **11 to 19 years** (known as the 'teenage' years).
   - **Puberty:** The developmental phase marked by hormonal activation during which the human body undergoes physical transformation and achieves sexual maturity / reproductive competence.
2. **Secondary Sexual Characteristics:**
   - **Boys:** Growth of facial hair (moustache, beard), chest hair, deepening of voice due to enlargement of the larynx (**Adam's Apple**), broadening of shoulders and muscular development.
   - **Girls:** Development of mammary glands (breasts), widening of hips/pelvis, beginning of the menstrual cycle (**menarche**), and softer, higher-pitched voice.
   - **Both:** Rapid height increase (**growth spurt**), development of pubic and underarm hair, increased activity of sweat and sebaceous (oil) glands leading to **acne/pimples**.
3. **Endocrine Glands & Hormones:**
   - Chemical messengers secreted directly into the bloodstream by ductless glands:
     - **Testes:** Produce **Testosterone** (regulates sperm production and male secondary sexual characteristics).
     - **Ovaries:** Produce **Oestrogen** (regulates egg maturation, secondary female characteristics) and **Progesterone**.
     - **Pituitary Gland:** Known as the **Master Gland**; secretes growth hormone and stimulates gonads (testes and ovaries) to release their hormones.
     - **Thyroid:** Secretes **Thyroxine** (regulates metabolism; iodine deficiency causes **Goitre**).
     - **Pancreas:** Secretes **Insulin** (regulates blood glucose; deficiency causes Diabetes).
     - **Adrenal Glands:** Secrete **Adrenaline** (prepares the body for stress/'fight or flight').
4. **Reproductive Health & Menstrual Hygiene:**
   - The menstrual cycle recurs approximately every **28 to 30 days** in females.
   - Maintaining meticulous hygiene (using clean sanitary pads, changing them every 4–6 hours, safe disposal) prevents reproductive tract infections (RTIs).
5. **Holistic Well-being & Substance Abuse Prevention:**
   - **Balanced Diet:** Requires carbohydrates for energy, proteins for muscle development, calcium for bone density, and **iron** (spinach, jaggery, pulses) to prevent anaemia.
   - **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan:** A nationwide government movement advocating complete resistance against tobacco, alcohol, and narcotic drugs, which severely damage adolescent brains and physical development.
   - **Cyber Safety:** Guarding private personal information online, rejecting cyberbullying, and fostering positive digital habits.

## 🎯 Competency-Based Questions — Chapter 6 (Page 94)

### A. Case-Based Questions:
**Case:** A group of students notice that some friends feel worried about pimples and body odour. They decide to organise a 'Healthy Adolescence Week' at school.
- **Q1. What common physical changes related to adolescence did the students observe?**
  > **Answer:** Increased secretion of sweat glands (sudoriferous glands) and oil glands (sebaceous glands), which produces body odour and acne/pimples on the face.
- **Q2. List two hygiene tips that can be shared during the campaign.**
  > **Answer:**
  > 1. Take a daily bath with mild soap and wear clean, breathable cotton undergarments to prevent bacterial breakdown of sweat that causes body odour.
  > 2. Wash the face twice daily with clean water and mild face wash without aggressively squeezing or picking at pimples to avoid infections and scars.
- **Q3. Why is it important to openly discuss issues like body odour and acne?**
  > **Answer:** Open discussion normalizes these physiological changes as natural biological consequences of puberty, dispels groundless shame and anxiety, and equips students with scientific hygiene practices to care for their bodies confidently.

### C. Higher Order Thinking Skills (HOTS):
1. **Mary and Manoj were classmates and good friends. Mary developed a little bulge on the front of her neck and visited a doctor, who advised an iodine-rich diet. Manoj developed a bump on his throat, but the doctor told him it was a normal part of growing up. What is the possible reason for advising Mary and Manoj differently? [NCERT]**
   > **Answer:**
   > - **Mary's Condition:** Mary developed swelling of the **thyroid gland (Goitre)** situated in front of the neck, caused by deficiency of dietary iodine needed to produce thyroxine hormone. Hence, she was prescribed an iodine-rich diet (iodised salt, seafood).
   > - **Manoj's Condition:** Manoj developed an enlarged protruding larynx (**Adam's Apple**), which is a normal secondary sexual characteristic in adolescent boys caused by testosterone, leading to voice deepening. It is not a disease and requires no medical treatment.
2. **How can understanding hormones help adolescents accept physical body changes with confidence?**
   > **Answer:** When adolescents recognize that bodily transformations—such as growth spurts, voice cracking, acne, body hair, and emotional mood swings—are universally orchestrated by temporary hormonal adjustments rather than personal flaws, it alleviates anxiety, eliminates self-consciousness, and fosters body positivity and healthy self-esteem.
